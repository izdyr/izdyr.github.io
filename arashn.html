Before you buy or rent anything
Where the sound is made. Nothing touches a reed. You shape a column of air across an open hole and the edge splits it. That is why the first six weeks are almost entirely tone, and why a beginner sounds like wind before they sound like music.
Keys and fingering. Student flutes use closed keys, so a fingertip that lands slightly off still seals. Open-hole models come later, when the hand is big enough and the ear is ready for the tuning control they allow.
The footjoint. A C foot is standard. A B foot adds one low note and a little weight, and it matters for orchestral repertoire not for a first instrument. Nobody needs to pay for it in year one.
The whole instrument. Around 67 cm in three pieces, and light. The real constraint is arm span and breath, not strength, which is why most students start between eight and ten. Rent before you buy. We will tell you honestly at the trial whether the hands are ready.
